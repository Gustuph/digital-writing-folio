# First ideas - Wednesday April 15th

### Progress Audit Documentation

My early concept for my work of E-Lit is an exploration of the way grief, regret, and guilt can paralyse somebody and keep them from acknowledging or confronting their troubles. This primarily stemmed from the idea of a character having to work through trauma by moving through a house that is haunted by a ghost who changed the setting every time you entered and exited a room. The story will entirely take place in the manor that is being haunted, so I've completed a very simple initial layout of the house on Miro, visible here: https://au.miro.com/app/board/u3jVOfxPrsM=/?share_link_id=896997658275. 

The brainstorming I've done outside of this is largely around the story, where I'm playing with the idea of a character who feels responsible for the death of their child. I have a reservation about this idea, however, as I worry about finding the right line where the reader upon realising this conflict is still on the narrator's side, while holding some understanding for the challenge of overcoming. I do think the character's guilt as they work their way through the manor could be compelling as they willfully decieve the reader and keep them from getting a clearer picture of the story. All while the atmosphere of a haunted house keeps the reader on tenterhooks. I think it could make for a very interesting story.

My biggest worry with this idea is that the interactivity might not be necessarily strong enough for the constraints and requirements of this task. Can the investigation be enough to cover this? I think potentially adding in an information collection element to this could potentially help satisfy this, as it simultaneously works to create a clearer picture for the reader of what guilt the narrator is trying to hide from.

Brainstorming over the weekend, I think I want to shift the idea a bit to an arsonist and a fire instead of a bomb. I think distracts the audience from the themes less and allows me to pose the surreal experience of investigating a cinema that burnt down but it still looks fine. I'm thinking of really leaning into this, by having all of the witnesses hanging around in the cinema too. I don't think I'm a good enough artist to employ any real visuals, so this might just need to be a text-based game, but potentially the sound of crackling fire growing as the witness gives their statement could amplify this experience?

Another point I wanted to consider was there being a ghost in each of the rooms and by figuring out details of their life, you can interact with them and they can give you more information. The last ghost you'll interact with is the protagonist's loved one. While the activity in this space will be the most violent, I think I want it to be the only room that isn't being haunted, and the protagonist is just projecting their guilt into the space where their loved one died.

## What is the purpose of your piece? Was there an inspiration that brought it about?

This piece started with the idea of being haunted, of a ghost that followed you through the story. Expanding this piece into an exploration of themes of grief and finding forgiveness for yourself felt like the natural extension of haunting. 

## What is the overall vision for your piece (spatially, architecturally) and how do the prototyped four rooms/screens fit into that vision? Have you received feedback on anything that is missing or unclear, that you need to develop further?

I want it to function as an investigation into the events of a fire at a cinema where the protagonist lost a loved one and is struggling to confront their grief and their inability to help them. The protagnist will move through four spaces, interacting with witnesses to learn the identities of the ghost that haunts each space, choosing to either forgive them for their role in the fire, or condemn them, potentially making the room inaccessable. This eventually forces the protagonist to confront the blame they are projecting onto their loved one's ghost, where the decisions to forgive or condemn the other ghosts influences whether or not they can forgive themselves.

The four rooms I'll be using for my prototype presentation will probably be interactions with witnesses in the early stage of the investigation. After consulting with peers, I think this makes more sense than using just the four literal spaces, as the core of the piece is in the witness interaction and the information gathering.
